# About Bluebird Bakery

Source URL: https://bluebird-bakery.example/about
Recorded on: 2026-09-14

Bluebird Bakery is owned and run by two people, Hannah Kowalski and Pete Kowalski. We are married, we
are the co-owners, and between us we do every job in the building.

## How it started

Hannah spent nine years baking in other people's kitchens, the last four of them on the overnight
bread shift at a hotel downtown. Pete taught middle school math. In 2015 a friend moved away and left
a jar of sourdough starter in our fridge with a note that said "feed me." Hannah fed it. By the end of
that year she was baking twelve loaves every Saturday in our home oven and Pete was delivering them to
neighbors on a bicycle.

In 2016 the lease came up on a narrow blue building in Lincoln Square that had been a shoe repair shop for
forty years. We signed it, spent a summer scrubbing, and opened that September with one secondhand deck
oven and a line of neighbors who already knew the bread.

## One location, on purpose

We have had one location since 2016, and we intend to keep it that way. We have been asked to open a
second shop twice, once in Round Rock and once inside a food hall downtown, and we said no both times.
We have also turned down outside investment. Nothing against growing. We just know that the bread is
good because Hannah's hands are on every loaf, and Hannah only has two hands.

So: one shop, at 15 Example Street, Chicago, IL 60625. No second counter, no stall at a market, no
truck. If you see Bluebird bread for sale somewhere else, somebody bought it from us that morning and
is being generous or enterprising.

## Who does what

**Hannah Kowalski, co-owner and baker.** Starts at 3am. Mixes, shapes and bakes all of the bread,
laminates the pastry dough, and decorates every whole cake herself. Happiest when nobody talks to her
before the first bake is out.

**Pete Kowalski, co-owner, counter and books.** Opens the doors at 7am, pulls the espresso, answers
the phone, and writes the cake orders in the book by the register. Does payroll for a staff of two,
which he describes as the easiest math he has ever taught.

On Saturdays Hannah's niece helps at the counter for a few hours. Otherwise, if you have been served
at Bluebird, one of the two of us served you.

## What we care about

- **Flour.** Stone-milled Illinois wheat from a mill in the Hill Country, plus a little rye.
- **Time.** The country loaf takes about thirty hours from mixing to the shelf. The croissants take
  three days.
- **Selling out.** We would rather run out at noon than throw bread away at closing. What little is
  left goes to a food pantry a few blocks north.
- **Saying things plainly.** When prices go up, we say so. When we cannot do something, such as
  wholesale, gluten-friendly baking or wedding cakes, we say that too and point you to someone who
  can.
