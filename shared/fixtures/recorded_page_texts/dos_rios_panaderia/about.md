# About Dos Rios Panaderia

Source URL: https://dos-rios-panaderia.example/about
Recorded on: 2026-09-14

Dos Rios Panaderia is owned by Elena Villarreal. She opened it in 2009 and she is still the first
person through the back door most mornings. It is a family business in the plain sense of the words:
her mother taught her the recipes, her husband keeps the ovens running, and two of her three children
work the counter.

## Where the name comes from

Elena grew up in a small town in Nuevo León where two rivers meet at the edge of the plaza, and the
town's one panaderia sat on the corner closest to the water. Her mother, Doña Carmela, worked there for
twenty-two years and baked at home on top of that. When the family moved to Chicago in 1998, the
recipes came along in a school notebook that still sits on a shelf over the mixing bench, held
together with packing tape.

Chicago has a river too. When it came time to paint a name on the window, "Dos Rios" was the only one
anybody in the family suggested.

## How the panaderia started

For eleven years Elena worked in other people's kitchens in Chicago, first washing sheet pans, then
shaping bolillos on the night shift at a wholesale bakery, then running the pastry side of a grocery
store bakery on the east side. She saved for an oven the whole time. In 2009 a unit opened up in a
shopping strip in South Shore, six blocks from the family's house, and she signed the lease the same
week.

The first year, the shop sold four things: conchas, bolillos, empanadas de calabaza and coffee. The
cases were half empty by design, because there was one baker and one oven. Today there are more than
thirty kinds of pan dulce, two deck ovens, a cold case, and a crew of nine, most of whom live within a
mile of the shop.

## One shop

We have one shop. It is the same one we opened in 2009, at 32 Example Street in South Shore, and
it is the only place our bread is baked or sold. We have been asked about opening on the north side
more than once. Elena's answer has not changed: she wants to be able to taste everything that goes
into the case, and she can only be in one kitchen at three in the morning.

## The family

- **Elena Villarreal**, owner and head baker. Mixes the concha dough herself, every day.
- **Doña Carmela**, Elena's mother. Retired, officially. Still comes in on Fridays to make the
  capirotada during Lent and to tell everyone the conchas were bigger in her day.
- **Rodrigo Villarreal**, Elena's husband. Fixes the ovens, drives to the flour supplier, and makes
  the café de olla.
- **Marisol and Mateo Villarreal**, Elena's daughter and son. Marisol runs the front of the shop and
  takes the photos for our Instagram. Mateo works the counter on weekends while he finishes community
  college.

## What we care about

We bake four times a day so that bread is warm in the afternoon and not only at dawn. We make our own
fillings: the pumpkin is cooked down with piloncillo and cinnamon, and the cajeta takes most of a
morning. We keep prices where a family can fill a tray on a Sunday. And we close at three on Sundays
so that the people who work here can have dinner with their own families.

## In the neighborhood

Every year we send pan dulce to the teacher breakfast at the elementary school down the street, and
bolillos to the parish kitchen on the last Saturday of the month. If you run something in Dove
Springs and think we can help, write to Elena at elena@dos-rios-panaderia.example.
