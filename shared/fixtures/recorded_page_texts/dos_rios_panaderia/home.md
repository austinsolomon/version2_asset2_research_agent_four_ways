# Dos Rios Panaderia — pan dulce baked every morning in South Shore

Source URL: https://dos-rios-panaderia.example/
Recorded on: 2026-09-14

Bienvenidos. Dos Rios Panaderia is a family panaderia in the South Shore part of southeast Chicago. The
ovens come on at three in the morning, the first trays of conchas reach the cases a little before six,
and the smell gets to the parking lot before you do.

## Hours

- Monday to Friday: 6am to 7pm
- Saturday: 6am to 8pm
- Sunday: 6am to 3pm

We close on Christmas Day and on New Year's Day. On the Day of the Dead, on Three Kings Day and on the
morning of Mother's Day we open at 5am, because on those mornings the line starts early.

## Where we are

32 Example Street, Chicago, IL 60649. We are in the small shopping strip with the yellow sign, between
the laundromat and the tire shop. Park anywhere in the lot out front. The city bus stops at the corner.

## What comes out of the ovens

**Pan dulce.** More than thirty kinds on a normal day: conchas, cuernitos, orejas, marranitos,
polvorones, empanadas filled with pumpkin, pineapple or sweet cream, besos, campechanas and
cinnamon-sugar donas. The full list with prices is on the [Menu](https://dos-rios-panaderia.example/menu)
page.

**Bolillos and teleras.** Baked four times a day, at 6am, 10am, 2pm and 5pm, so the rolls you take home
for dinner are still warm. The taquerias and torta shops on our side of town have known that schedule
for years.

**From the cold case.** Tres leches by the slice, flan, gelatinas in layered colors, arroz con leche
and chocoflan on weekends.

**Seasonal.** Pan de muerto from the middle of October to the second of November. Rosca de reyes from
the day after Christmas until the sixth of January. Capirotada on Fridays during Lent. Buñuelos in
December.

**Drinks.** Café de olla, champurrado in the cold months, and aguas frescas in the hot ones, which in
Chicago is most of them.
