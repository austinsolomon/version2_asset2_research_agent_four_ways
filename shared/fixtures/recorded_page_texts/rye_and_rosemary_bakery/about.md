# About Rye & Rosemary Bakery

Source URL: https://rye-and-rosemary.example/about
Recorded on: 2026-09-14

Rye & Rosemary Bakery is owned by Sofia Marchetti, who is also the head baker. She opened the shop in
2019 and she is still the first person through the door every morning.

## How it started

Sofia grew up above her grandparents' bread shop in a hill town outside Bologna, where the rosemary
grew wild along the road and ended up in most of what the family baked. She came to Chicago in 2009 to
study, stayed, and spent eight years baking for restaurants around the city, the last three running
the bread program for a hotel kitchen downtown.

In 2018 she started selling a rye and rosemary sourdough from a folding table at a Saturday farmers
market. It sold out by nine every week. In the spring of 2019 a former florist's shop came up for
lease in Roscoe Village, with a big front window and a back room just large enough for a deck oven. She
signed the lease in April and opened in June.

## One shop in Roscoe Village

We have one shop, at 27 Example Street, Chicago, IL 60657, and everything we sell is baked there. We
left the farmers market in 2020, because one oven cannot fill a shop and a market stall on the same
morning. We have no second location, no stall and no truck, and we have no plans for any of them. We
also do not sell wholesale, apart from the bread for one restaurant around the corner whose chef gave
Sofia her first job in this city.

## Who is here

- **Sofia Marchetti, owner and head baker.** Mixes and shapes the sourdough, decides what goes in the
  case, and signs the checks. If you have a question about the business itself, Sofia is the person to
  write to: sofia@rye-and-rosemary.example.
- **Two bakers on the bench.** One has been here since the first week. They handle the laminated dough
  and the focaccia.
- **Three people at the counter,** in turns. They bag the bread, pour the coffee, answer the phone and
  keep the clipboard of orders by the register.

That is seven of us on the busiest week of the year and six the rest of the time.

## How we bake

Every loaf is naturally leavened. The starter is fed twice a day and is older than the shop. The rye is
stone-milled in Illinois. The rosemary grows in the planters by the front door, and yes, people do pick
it on their way past, and no, we do not mind.

We bake what one oven can bake. That is about ninety loaves on a weekday and a hundred and sixty on a
Saturday, and when they are gone we do not have a second batch hiding in the back. It is the reason
the home page talks so much about ordering ahead.

## What we are not good at

We will be honest: the baking is the part of this business that works. The rest is a clipboard, a
shared email inbox and a phone that rings while everybody's hands are full. Sofia built this website
herself over a weekend in 2019 and has changed it twice since, both times to update the hours. Regulars
ask why they cannot just put in an order from their phone at night, the way they can with a pizza. It
is a fair question, and the honest answer is that nobody here has had the time to work out how.

## In the neighborhood

We give unsold bread to a food pantry in west Chicago at the end of every day. We bake the rolls for the
elementary school's fall supper. Once a year, in October, Sofia teaches a Sunday sourdough class for
twelve people in the back room; the sign-up sheet is a piece of paper taped to the register.
